TAAI 2026 主題演講 I · 第 1 天 2026 年 11 月 20 日（週五） · 09:30–10:20

Beyond Words: Riemannian Geometry for Next-Generation Graph Foundation Models

Prof. Philip S. Yu

伊利諾大學芝加哥分校 傑出教授暨 Wexler 資訊科技講座教授（UIC Distinguished Professor and Wexler Chair in Information Technology, Department of Computer Science, University of Illinois Chicago）· ACM / IEEE / AAAS Fellow

摘要（Abstract）

Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) are poised to transform graph learning, yet substantial debate remains over how to construct a general-purpose GFM analogous to Large Language Models (LLMs). Traditional Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) struggle with memory retention, principled interpretability, and multi-domain adaptation, while graph serialization limits the direct application of LLMs, as linear tokens fail to capture the rich structural complexity of graphs. In contrast, Riemannian geometry provides a natural, mathematically principled framework for modeling graph structures, offering compatibility with semantic graph learning and LLM integration. In this talk, we argue that for graphs, geometry speaks louder than words. We introduce Riemannian Graph Foundations, which prioritize intrinsic graph geometry and endow models with endogenous capabilities for structural inference and generation, moving beyond simple representation-space transformations. Recent results on building a graph foundation model within Riemannian geometric space will be presented, highlighting how this approach lays the groundwork for next-generation graph intelligence.

講者簡介（Biography）

Dr. Philip S. Yu is a Fellow of the ACM, IEEE, and AAAS. Dr. Yu received the ACM SIGKDD 2016 Innovation Award for his influential research and scientific contributions to mining, fusion, and anonymization of big data; the IEEE Computer Society 2013 Technical Achievement Award for “pioneering and fundamentally innovative contributions to the scalable indexing, querying, searching, mining, and anonymization of big data”; and the Research Contributions Award from ICDM in 2003 for his pioneering work in data mining. He has published over 2,000 refereed conference and journal papers, which have been cited more than 278,000 times, with an H-index of 223. He has also applied for more than 300 patents. Dr. Yu served as Editor-in-Chief of ACM TKDD (2011–2017) and IEEE TKDE (2001–2004).

TAAI 2026 主題演講 II · 第 1 天 2026 年 11 月 20 日（週五） · 16:10–17:00

Neural Speech Enhancement and Assessment and Their Applications in Assistive Oral Communication Technologies

曹昱 研究員（Prof. Yu Tsao）

中央研究院 研究員（Research Fellow, Academia Sinica, Taiwan）

摘要（Abstract）

This presentation is divided into three parts. Firstly, we will discuss our recent advancements in neural speech enhancement (SE), a critical element in various speech-related applications. The primary objective of SE is to enhance speech signals by mitigating distortions caused by additive and convoluted noises, thereby improving human-human and human-machine communication efficacy. We'll delve into the system architecture and fundamental theories behind neural SE approaches, as well as explore important directions aimed at achieving better performance. Moving on to the second part, we will focus on our recent progress in neural speech assessment (SA), which aims to effectively evaluate the quality and intelligibility of spoken audio—a crucial aspect in numerous speech-related applications. Traditionally, the evaluation process often relies on listening tests involving human participants, which can be both resource-intensive and impractical due to the need for a large number of listeners. To address this challenge, neural SA metrics have garnered notable attention. We will discuss the fundamental systems of neural SA, highlight several factors influencing performance, and explore emerging trends in this domain. Finally, we will present some applications of neural SE and SA in assistive oral communication technologies. These applications include impaired speech transformation and noise reduction for assistive hearing and speaking devices. Through these discussions, our aim is to illustrate the potential impact of neural-based approaches in improving communication accessibility for individuals with oral communication disorders.

講者簡介（Biography）

Yu Tsao (Senior Member, IEEE) received the B.S. and M.S. degrees in Electrical Engineering from National Taiwan University, Taipei, Taiwan, in 1999 and 2001, respectively, and the Ph.D. degree in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the Georgia Institute of Technology, Atlanta, GA, USA, in 2008. From 2009 to 2011, he was a Researcher at the National Institute of Information and Communications Technology (NICT), Tokyo, Japan, where he conducted research and product development in multilingual speech-to-speech translation systems, focusing on automatic speech recognition. He is currently a Research Fellow (Professor) and the Deputy Director at the Research Center for Information Technology Innovation, Academia Sinica, Taipei, Taiwan. He also holds a joint appointment as a Professor in the Department of Electrical Engineering at Chung Yuan Christian University, Taoyuan, Taiwan. His research interests include assistive oral communication technologies, audio coding, and bio-signal processing. He serves as an Associate Editor for IEEE Transactions on Consumer Electronics and IEEE Signal Processing Letters. He received the Outstanding Research Award from Taiwan’s National Science and Technology Council (NSTC), the 2025 IEEE Chester W. Sall Memorial Award, and served as the corresponding author of a paper that won the 2021 IEEE Signal Processing Society Young Author Best Paper Award.

TAAI 2026 主題演講 III · 第 2 天 2026 年 11 月 21 日（週六） · 09:00–09:50

AI-Driven Network Traffic Analysis for Anomaly Detection and Cybersecurity

Prof. Ljiljana Trajkovic

加拿大西門菲莎大學 教授（Simon Fraser University, Canada）· IEEE Fellow

摘要（Abstract）

Collection and analysis of data from deployed networks is essential for understanding communication networks. Hence, data mining and statistical analysis of network data have been employed to determine traffic loads, analyze patterns of users' behavior, predict future network traffic, and detect traffic anomalies. The Internet has historically been prone to failures and attacks that significantly degrade its performance, affect the Internet connectivity, and cause routing disconnections. Frequent cases of various cyber threats have been encountered over the years and, hence, detection of anomalous behavior is a topic of great interest in cybersecurity. In described case studies, traffic traces collected by various collection sites are used to classify network anomalies. Various anomaly and intrusion detection approaches based on machine learning have been employed to analyze collected data. Deep learning, broad learning, gradient boosted decision trees, and reservoir computing algorithms were used to develop models based on collected datasets that contain Internet worms, viruses, power outages, ransomware events, router misconfigurations, Internet Protocol hijacks, and infrastructure failures in times of conflict. The reported results indicate that while performance of machine learning models greatly depends on the used datasets, they are viable tools for detecting the Internet anomalies.

講者簡介（Biography）

Ljiljana Trajkovic received the Dipl. Ing. degree from University of Pristina, Yugoslavia, the M.Sc. degrees in electrical engineering and computer engineering from Syracuse University, Syracuse, NY, and the Ph.D. degree in electrical engineering from University of California at Los Angeles. She is currently a professor in the School of Engineering Science, Simon Fraser University, Burnaby, British Columbia, Canada. Her research interests include communication networks and dynamical systems. Dr. Trajkovic served as IEEE Division X Delegate/Director, President of the IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Society, and President of the IEEE Circuits and Systems Society. She serves as Editor-in-Chief of the IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems. She is a Distinguished Lecturer of the IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Society and was a Distinguished Lecturer of the IEEE Circuits and System Society. She is a Fellow of the IEEE.

TAAI 2026 主題演講 IV · 第 2 天 2026 年 11 月 21 日（週六） · 15:40–16:30

Getting to Know You: Long- and Short-Term Adaptation in Dialogue Systems

Prof. Kazunori Komatani

日本大阪大學產業科學研究所 教授（SANKEN, The University of Osaka, Japan）

摘要（Abstract）

Large language models have made dialogue systems capable of generating fluent responses. However, being good with words is not the same as understanding the person one is interacting with. A dialogue agent should adapt not just to any user, but to the particular person it is talking to, whose knowledge, experiences, and feelings differ from those of others. In this talk, I will discuss our research on user-adaptive spoken dialogue systems from two complementary perspectives. The first is acquiring knowledge through interaction, particularly learning unfamiliar words by deciding when, what, and how to ask users. The second is understanding users' current states through multimodal sentiment estimation, using information from speech, facial expressions, and physiological signals. I will also discuss how these research topics relate to recent developments in agentic AI, particularly from the perspectives of state representation and action selection.

講者簡介（Biography）

Kazunori Komatani is a Professor at SANKEN, the University of Osaka, Japan. He received his Doctor of Informatics degree from Kyoto University in 2002. He was an Assistant Professor at Kyoto University from 2002 to 2010 and an Associate Professor at Nagoya University from 2010 to 2014. He was also a Visiting Scientist at Carnegie Mellon University from 2008 to 2009. He joined SANKEN as a Professor in 2014. His research interests include spoken dialogue systems, dialogue management, user adaptation, and multimodal sentiment analysis.

Q1. 議程已經確定了嗎？

尚未。目前為暫定議程，場次分配、場次名稱、主持人與時間仍可能調整，請以本頁最新資訊為準。

Q2. 如何找到我的論文？

請至論文發表場次，依論文編號（例如 148）、題目、作者或關鍵字搜尋。可用搜尋範圍只搜尋單一欄位，並用主題、日期與場次類型篩選。每篇論文都有專屬連結（例如 program.html#paper-148），可分享給共同作者。

Q3. 我的論文編號在哪裡？

論文編號即您在 OpenReview（外部網站，另開新分頁） 的投稿編號，也列在論文發表場次每篇論文旁（例如「論文 #148」）。來信詢問論文相關事項時，請務必註明。

Q4. 我的論文資訊有誤，或找不到我的論文，該怎麼辦？

請於 2026 年 11 月 9 日前來信 taai2026org@gmail.com 並註明論文編號。請確認論文題目、作者姓名、場次與發表時間（海報論文請一併確認看板編號）。

Q5. 可以更換場次或發表時間嗎？我的時間有衝突。

場次安排已避免同一作者有兩篇論文在同一時段發表，發表形式（口頭或海報）由大會決定。若仍有時間衝突，請於 2026 年 11 月 9 日前來信 taai2026org@gmail.com，註明論文編號與原因。

Q6. 議程上的時間是哪個時區？

本頁所有時間皆為台北時間（UTC+8）。

Q7. 我有多篇錄取論文，在議程中如何安排？

每篇論文各有發表時段；在論文發表場次將搜尋範圍設為「作者」，即可看到您所有的論文。每篇錄取論文也須各自報名，且至少有一位作者現場發表。詳見報名頁 FAQ Q4。

Q8. Program & Abstract Book 是什麼？

彙整大會議程與論文題目、作者、服務單位、摘要及場次資訊的文件，性質為會議發表紀錄，而非正式論文集。詳見徵稿頁 FAQ Q33。

Q9. 會提供紙本大會手冊嗎？

不提供。為符合永續，TAAI 2026 不列印紙本大會手冊或 Program & Abstract Book，兩者皆僅提供 PDF 數位版。大會手冊可於上方下載。最新議程（包括手冊發布後的異動）以本頁為準。

Q10. 為什麼論文發表場次中沒有高中生、特別場次或產業軌道的論文？

論文發表場次列出國際與國內論文組的口頭及海報論文。議程總表中的其他場次，例如高中生口頭場次（第 2 天，會議室 C）、特別場次、產業軌道及 TAAI 碩博士論文獎口頭報告，另行安排；時間與地點請見議程總表。

Q11. 口頭發表有多少時間？

每篇約 10 分鐘（含問答），依各場次列表順序發表；每篇的開始時間列在論文發表場次。

Q12. 口頭發表前需要做什麼準備？

請在論文發表場次確認您的場次、會議室與開始時間，並於場次開始前到場。論文依列表順序發表，建議全程參與該場次。各場次主持人確認後將列於場次資訊中。

Q13. 口頭發表提供哪些設備？投影片如何準備？

每間會議室提供投影機與簡報筆電（Windows，可開啟 PowerPoint 與 PDF），以 HDMI 連接。投影片請採 16:9，存成 PDF 或 PPTX，並於場次開始前的休息時間以 USB 隨身碟拷貝到現場筆電；建議另備一份 PDF。若使用自己的筆電，請自備 HDMI 轉接頭（例如 USB-C 轉 HDMI），並於場次開始前測試。

Q14. 發表要使用哪種語言？

國際場次以英文發表。國內場次可使用中文或英文發表；建議投影片使用英文，方便國際與會者理解。

Q15. 我的論文為何被安排為口頭或海報發表？

發表形式由大會根據審查結果及議程安排決定。口頭與海報是不同的論文類型，而非不同的審查標準：國際論文組的口頭論文以完整論文（12–15 頁）出版、海報論文以短篇論文（6–11 頁）出版，皆收錄於同一 Springer CCIS post-proceedings。國內延伸摘要原則上以海報形式發表。詳見徵稿頁 FAQ Q19 與 Q29。

Q16. 海報發表需要注意什麼？

每篇海報論文有固定看板編號：IP- 為國際論文、DP- 為國內論文，列在論文發表場次的論文旁。海報請採 A0 直式（A1 亦可，不接受橫式）。請自行印製並攜帶海報，大會提供看板與固定用品。請於場次開始前完成張貼，並於場次期間在場說明與回答問題；張貼與撤除時間將另行通知。詳見海報發表規範。

Q17. 海報何時張貼、何時撤除？

每個海報場次都有各自的看板，並標示看板編號（IP-／DP-）。請於該場次開始前的休息時間，在您的看板完成張貼，並於場次結束後立即撤除，以便下一場次使用。大會提供固定用品；看板尺寸與張貼細節將公告於海報區。

Q18. 我選擇 Presentation Only（NOT-to-Publish），論文還會在議程中嗎？

會。Presentation Only 代表論文不收錄於論文集，不會改變口頭或海報的安排，也不會取消發表時段；仍須至少一位作者完成報名並現場發表。題目、作者與摘要仍可能列於議程資料中。詳見完稿頁 FAQ Q7 與徵稿頁 FAQ Q12。

Q19. 可以由學生或其他共同作者代為發表嗎？

可以。發表作者報名須由實際現場發表的作者持有，因此由該共同作者以本人名義報名；報名費可由您代為支付。詳見報名頁 FAQ Q19。

Q20. 可以遠距發表嗎？

Q21. 如果沒有作者發表，論文會如何處理？

每篇錄取論文須至少有一位已報名的作者現場發表（或經事先核准以遠距發表）。未發表的論文可能自議程中移除，且無法出版。若沒有作者能出席，請儘早來信 taai2026org@gmail.com。詳見完稿頁 FAQ Q24 與報名頁 FAQ Q24。

Q22. 各場次的主持人是誰？

場次主持人仍在確認中，確認後將列於論文發表場次的各場次資訊中（目前顯示為「待公布」）。

Q23. 我是場次主持人，需要做什麼？

請於場次開始前約 10 分鐘到場，確認發表者到齊、投影片已拷貝，介紹每位講者，控制每篇時間（約 8 分鐘報告＋2 分鐘問答）並主持問答；場次結束後，請將未發表的論文回報報到處。發表者請於場次開始前先向主持人報到。
